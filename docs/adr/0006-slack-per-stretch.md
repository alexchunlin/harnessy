# Slack lives on each stretch between fixed points, and a stored length stays the routed length

The first map ruled slack out because yarn-and-rope lengths carried their own margin. Lengths now come from the 3D view, which measures a tight path, so the BOM would cut every wire short. Alex's feedback on 2026-10-09 asked for slack tuned per stretch of a run: a Z-shaped run from a connector through two clips to another connector might carry 10 mm at the top, none between the clips and 20 mm at the bottom. Every endpoint of a segment bounds a stretch, and so does every tie point, so a stretch always sits inside one segment. A tie point carries the slack of the stretch on its first-end side, and the segment carries the slack of its last stretch, which is the whole segment when it has no tie points. Removing a tie point merges its stretch into the next one and sums the two slacks, so the cut length does not move. Adding a tie point splits a stretch and the new tie point starts with no slack of its own. A stretch with no value takes the harness's default from the anchor segment, else the project's default, which starts at zero so existing projects keep their lengths. Slack is in millimetres and never negative. A conductor's cut length is the sum over its segments of the routed length plus every stretch's slack, whether the length was typed by hand or written from 3D. A cable row and a sheath row get slack the same way. A purchased assembly has none; the inspector hides the field and a design rule warning flags a file that carries one. A warning also flags a stretch whose slack exceeds its routed length. The 3D view prints the routed length and the segment's total slack beside each tube; the topology inspector lists a segment's stretches with a slack field each. The decision was made on the ticket [What is slack, what does it attach to, and how does the BOM apply it?](https://github.com/alexchunlin/harnessy/issues/32).

## Considered options

- Folding slack into the stored length, so a segment's length is already the cut length. Rejected: the 3D view overwrites the length on every write, and nobody could tell afterwards how much of a number was path and how much was margin.
- One slack per segment. Rejected: it cannot express the Z-shaped run, which is the case Alex described.
- A list of slacks on the segment, one per stretch in order. Rejected: dragging a tie point past another in 3D silently reassigns every value in the list.
- A stretch stored as its own record with an id. Rejected: there is nothing to say about a stretch beyond its bounds and its slack, and the id would have to be kept in step with every tie point edit and 3D write.
- Slack per net. Rejected: every conductor in a bundle crosses the same stretch, so per-net slack would cut different lengths for wires tied together, and no BOM row could explain it.
- Slack as a percentage of routed length. Rejected: a clip-to-clip stretch wants a few millimetres regardless of how long it is, and the floor thinks in millimetres.
